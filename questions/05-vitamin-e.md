## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: The vitamers

How many vitamers of vitamin E are there? Name the two families, say which vitamer is most active, and say whether the body can convert one vitamer into another.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Eight vitamers.

Two families: tocopherols (α, β, γ, δ) and tocotrienols (α, β, γ, δ).

α-tocopherol is the most active.

They cannot interconvert.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: α-tocopherol equivalents

An oil contains, per 100 g: 30 mg α-tocopherol, 8 mg β-tocopherol, 40 mg γ-tocopherol and 10 mg α-tocotrienol.

How many mg α-tocopherol equivalents is that?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: weight each vitamer**

α-tocopherol: 30 × 1 = 30

β-tocopherol: 8 × 0.5 = 4

γ-tocopherol: 40 × 0.1 = 4

α-tocotrienol: 10 × 0.3 = 3

**Step 2: add up**

30 + 4 + 4 + 3 = **41 mg α-TE**

From: §2, Equation 1

</details>

### Q3: Free radicals

What is a free radical? Explain how one free radical can cause a cascade of damage, and name three things in the cell that free radicals damage.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: what it is**

A molecule with an unpaired electron. That makes it highly reactive.

**Step 2: the cascade**

It steals an electron from a stable molecule. That molecule now has an unpaired electron, so it is a new radical. It attacks the next molecule, and so on.

**Step 3: damage**

Any three of: cell membranes, DNA, RNA, proteins.

From: §5

</details>

---

### Q4: How vitamin E works

Describe how vitamin E stops a free radical, why vitamin E does not itself become dangerous, and how it is regenerated.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: donate**

Vitamin E donates a hydrogen from its phenolic –OH group to the radical. The radical becomes a stable non-radical product.

**Step 2: stay safe**

Vitamin E becomes the tocopheroxyl radical. This is stabilised by resonance in the aromatic ring, so it does not attack other molecules. The chain reaction ends.

**Step 3: recycle**

Vitamin C reduces the tocopheroxyl radical back to tocopherol.

From: §6

</details>

### Q5: Sources and requirement

Name three good food sources of vitamin E. State the adequate intake and explain why it is set the way it is.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any three of: sunflower oil, safflower oil, almonds, hazelnuts, other nuts, seeds, vegetable oils, green leafy vegetables.

AI = 0.4 mg α-TE per gram of dietary PUFA.

Vitamin E protects PUFA from oxidation. More PUFA in the diet means more vitamin E is needed.

From: §3

</details>

### Q6: Evidence, deficiency and toxicity

a. What do observational studies and randomised trials say about vitamin E and heart disease?

b. What are the signs of deficiency, and why can high doses cause bleeding?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** Epidemiology shows a clear association between high blood vitamin E and lower cardiovascular disease. But randomised controlled trials of high-dose supplements were disappointing: small effect, no effect, or increased health risks.

**b.** Deficiency is rare. Signs are neurological issues and haemolytic anaemia.
High doses may inhibit vitamin K, which is needed for clotting, so bleeding risk rises.

From: §8

</details>
