## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Name and sources

Where does the name pantothenic acid come from? Give four good food sources. In what two forms is it found in food, and which form makes up most of the intake?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: name**

From the Greek pantos, meaning everywhere. It is widely distributed in all foods.

**Step 2: sources**

Any four of: meats, eggs, yogurt, whole-grain cereals, sweet potatoes, broccoli. Also fortified cereal (about 10 mg), beef liver (about 6 mg), mushrooms (about 5 mg).

**Step 3: forms**

Free form, or as part of coenzyme A. About 85% of intake is as CoA.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Structure and CoA

What two parts make up pantothenic acid? What is added to it to make coenzyme A? Where does the acetyl group attach in acetyl-CoA?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: pantothenic acid**

Pantoic acid + β-alanine.

**Step 2: making CoA**

An SH group is added at one end. Adenosine 3,5-bisphosphate is added at the other.

**Step 3: acetyl-CoA**

The acetyl group attaches to the S of the SH group.

From: §2

</details>

---

### Q3: Metabolism

Describe what happens to the pantothenic acid in food from the gut to the tissues. Which tissues have the highest CoA concentrations?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: gut**

CoA in food is broken down to pantothenic acid, which is absorbed in the small intestine.

**Step 2: liver**

It circulates in the blood to the liver, where it is metabolised and circulated to the rest of the body.

**Step 3: tissues**

Most is used to make CoA. CoA is in high concentrations in the liver, heart, kidneys and brain.

From: §3

</details>

### Q4: CoA in catabolism and synthesis

a. Explain how CoA acts as a carrier of fatty acids during β-oxidation.

b. Give the first step of long chain fatty acid synthesis.

c. Name two other things CoA is needed to make.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** When fatty acids are broken down for energy, 2-carbon fragments are cut from the end of the chain. Each fragment joins CoA to form acetyl-CoA. Acetyl-CoA enters the TCA cycle for energy production.

**b.** Acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → malonyl-CoA.

**c.** Cholesterol and steroid hormones.

From: §4, §5

</details>

### Q5: The four energy vitamins compared

For each of thiamin, riboflavin, niacin and pantothenic acid, give the coenzyme form and the classic deficiency.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Vitamin | Coenzyme form | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Thiamin (B1) | TDP (TPP) | Beriberi. Wernicke-Korsakoff in alcoholics |
| Riboflavin (B2) | FAD, FMN | Cracked lips, sore throat, skin disorders, inflamed mouth |
| Niacin (B3) | NAD, NADP | Pellagra, the 4 Ds |
| Pantothenic acid (B5) | Coenzyme A | Burning feet syndrome |

All four are needed to turn pyruvate into acetyl-CoA and run the TCA cycle.

From: §6, Topic 6 §5, Topic 7 §1, Topic 7 §5

</details>
