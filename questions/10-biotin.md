### Q2: Carboxylases

What does a carboxylase enzyme do?

Give the example from the slides, naming the enzyme, the substrate and the product. Which pathway is it part of?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the job**

A carboxylase adds a carboxyl group to a compound.

**Step 2: the example**

Pyruvate carboxylase adds a carboxyl group to pyruvate to form oxaloacetate.

**Step 3: the pathway**

Glucose production, gluconeogenesis.

### Q3: Pathways

List the three areas of metabolism that depend on biotin. For fatty acid synthesis, name the enzyme and explain the link to pantothenic acid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the three areas**

Gluconeogenesis, fatty acid synthesis, amino acid metabolism.

**Step 2: fatty acid synthesis**

Acetyl-CoA carboxylase. It turns acetyl-CoA + CO₂ into malonyl-CoA, the first step of long chain fatty acid synthesis.

**Step 3: the link**

Acetyl-CoA contains coenzyme A, which is made from pantothenic acid. So the reaction needs biotin for the carboxylase and pantothenic acid for the CoA.

From: §3

</details>

### Q4: Deficiency

Why is biotin deficiency rare? What is the one well-known dietary cause, and how does it work? List the symptoms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: why rare**

Biotin is widely distributed in foods and gut bacteria synthesise it.

**Step 2: the cause**

Eating large amounts of uncooked eggs. Egg white contains avidin, a protein that binds tightly to biotin and prevents its absorption.

**Step 3: symptoms**

Dermatitis, hair loss, and neurological symptoms: lethargy, pins and needles in the extremities, depression.

From: §4

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Biotin is a coenzyme for carboxylase enzymes.
2. The EFSA adequate intake for biotin is 400 µg/day.
3. Cooked eggs cause biotin deficiency.
4. Biotin is synthesised by intestinal bacteria.
5. Pyruvate carboxylase converts pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **True.** (§3)
2. **False.** It is 40 µg/day. (§2)
3. **False.** Raw egg white, because of avidin. Cooking removes the problem. (§4)
4. **True.** (§2)
5. **False.** Pyruvate to oxaloacetate. Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA is pyruvate dehydrogenase, which needs thiamin. (§3)
