## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: What makes a vitamin a vitamin

Give four features that define a vitamin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any four of:

- Most are not made in the body, so they must come from the diet.
- Deficiency results in disease, and repletion cures it. This is the "vital amine" idea.
- Vitamins have no chemical structure in common.
- They do not yield energy.
- They are needed in small amounts, µg or mg.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Fat-soluble vs water-soluble

Compare fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins on three points: absorption, storage and excretion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: absorption**

Fat-soluble: into the lymph in chylomicrons, then the blood.

Water-soluble: directly into the blood.

**Step 2: storage**

Fat-soluble: stored in fat tissue and the liver.

Water-soluble: not stored, they circulate.

**Step 3: excretion**

Fat-soluble: less readily excreted, stay in fat storage.

Water-soluble: kidneys detect excess and remove it in the urine.

From: §2

</details>

### Q3: Dietary reference values

Define PRI, AR and AI. When is an AI used instead of a PRI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**PRI, Population Reference Intake:** the intake that is adequate for virtually all people in a population group.

**AR, Average Requirement:** the intake that is adequate for half the people in a population group, assuming a normal distribution of requirement.

**AI, Adequate Intake:** the average observed daily intake of a healthy population, assumed to be adequate.

An AI is used when a PRI cannot be set, because an average requirement cannot be determined.

From: §3

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Vitamins provide energy to the body.
2. Vitamin K is fat-soluble.
3. Water-soluble vitamins are stored in fat tissue.
4. The AR is the intake that covers virtually all people in a group.
5. Fat-soluble vitamins are absorbed into the lymph before the blood.
6. An AI is set when an average requirement cannot be determined.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **False.** Vitamins do not yield energy. (§1)
2. **True.** A, D, E and K are fat-soluble. (§2)
3. **False.** They circulate and excess is lost in urine. Fat-soluble vitamins are stored in fat. (§2)
4. **False.** That is the PRI. The AR covers half the group. (§3)
5. **True.** Chylomicrons carry them into the lymph, then the blood. (§2)
6. **True.** (§3)

</details>
