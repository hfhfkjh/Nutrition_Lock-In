## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Riboflavin coenzymes and structure

Name the two coenzymes made from riboflavin and give their full names. What is the side chain of riboflavin called, and where does it come from?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: coenzymes**

FMN = flavin mononucleotide.
FAD = flavin adenine dinucleotide.

**Step 2: side chain**

Ribitol. It comes from the reduction of ribose.

Flavus means yellow, which is why riboflavin is yellow.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Riboflavin functions

What type of reaction does riboflavin take part in? Give four places in metabolism where FAD is needed, and name the antioxidant enzyme that needs it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: reaction type**

Oxidation and reduction. FAD is an electron carrier: FAD → FADH₂.

**Step 2: where**

Mitochondrial electron transport chain. Fatty acid oxidation. Amino acid oxidation. TCA cycle.

**Step 3: antioxidant enzyme**

Glutathione reductase.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q3: Riboflavin deficiency

Describe the signs of riboflavin deficiency. Why might it lower the metabolic rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: signs**

Inflammatory lesions of the mouth: glossitis (inflamed tongue), cheilosis (cracked lips), stomatitis (inflamed mouth). Sore throat and skin disorders.

**Step 2: metabolic rate**

Lower metabolic rate, related to lipid metabolism. Fatty acid oxidation needs FAD, so without riboflavin fat cannot be burned properly.

From: §3, §4

</details>

---

### Q4: Niacin structure and coenzymes

Describe the structure of nicotinic acid. How does nicotinamide differ? Name the two coenzymes made from niacin and say how they differ from each other.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: nicotinic acid**

A pyridine ring with a carboxyl group (COOH) at position 3.

**Step 2: nicotinamide**

The carboxyl group is replaced by a carboxamide group (CONH₂).

**Step 3: coenzymes**

NAD (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) and NADP. NADP has the same structure as NAD with an extra phosphate.

From: §5

</details>

---

### Q5: Niacin equivalents

A meal contains 8 mg of preformed niacin and 420 mg of tryptophan.

How many mg niacin equivalents does it provide?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: niacin from tryptophan**

420 ÷ 60 = 7 mg

**Step 2: add the preformed niacin**

8 + 7 = **15 mg NE**

From: §6, Equation 1

</details>

---

### Q6: Niacin functions and pellagra

a. Give the two coenzyme roles of niacin and one non-redox role.

b. Why do populations living mainly on maize get pellagra?

c. List the 4 Ds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** NAD+ is the electron acceptor in energy-yielding metabolism (glycolysis, pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, TCA cycle, β-oxidation, feeding the ETC to make ATP). NADPH is the major coenzyme for reductive biosynthesis, including fatty acid synthesis and folate metabolism. Non-redox: DNA repair.

**b.** Maize is low in tryptophan, so little niacin can be made from it. The niacin present in cereals is in a bound form that is unavailable.

**c.** Dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia, death.

From: §6, §7, §8

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Riboflavin is yellow.
2. FAD is a coenzyme made from niacin.
3. Glutathione reductase needs riboflavin.
4. Nicotinamide has a carboxyl group at position 3.
5. 60 mg of tryptophan has the niacin activity of 1 mg of niacin.
6. Pellagra is caused by riboflavin deficiency.

1. **True.** Flavus = yellow. (§1)

4. **False.** Nicotinic acid has the carboxyl. Nicotinamide has a carboxamide (CONH₂). (§5)
5. **True.** (§6)
6. **False.** Niacin deficiency. (§8)

</details>
