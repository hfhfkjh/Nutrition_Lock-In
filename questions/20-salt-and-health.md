## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Salt and blood pressure

Explain the chain from sodium intake to cardiovascular disease, and name the three lifestyle targets public health campaigns use against it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: chain**

Sodium maintains blood volume. Blood volume is related to blood pressure. High salt intake is linked to hypertension. Hypertension is a risk factor for CVD.

**Step 2: targets**

Salt reduction, weight loss, stopping smoking.

### Q2: Label maths

1. A ready meal label says 1.1 g sodium per pack. How much salt is that, and what fraction of the 6 g target?
2. A woman eats 8.5 g salt a day. How much sodium is that?
3. The sandwich on the slides carries 1500 mg sodium. How much salt is that?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the rules**

Salt = 2.54 × sodium. Sodium = 0.4 × salt.

**Step 2: ready meal**

1.1 × 2.54 = **2.8 g salt**. 2.8 ÷ 6 = **0.47**, almost half the day's target.

**Step 3: the woman**

8.5 × 0.4 = **3.4 g sodium**.

**Step 4: sandwich**

1500 mg = 1.5 g sodium. 1.5 × 2.54 = **3.8 g salt**.

From: §2, Equation 1, §7

</details>

### Q4: Food groups and bread

Which three food groups supply more than half of Irish salt? What did the FSAI bread monitoring show, with numbers?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: groups**

Breads 22–25%, cured and processed meats 18%, meat and meat dishes 7–9%.

**Step 2: bread**

Salt in bread fell 18–30% between 2003 and 2012. White bread went from 1.4 to 1.1 g salt per 100 g. An 800 g loaf went from 11.2 g to 8.8–9.1 g salt. A family eating a loaf a day saves at least 800 g of salt a year (4.1 kg down to 3.3 kg).

From: §3, §4

</details>

### Q5: Salt sensitivity

What does salt-sensitive mean? Explain the ACE gene example: what ACE does, which variants matter, and the two consequences of having a salt-sensitive genotype.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: meaning**

Not everyone's blood pressure responds to salt. Salt-sensitive people are those whose blood pressure does respond. It depends on genetics and lifestyle.

**Step 2: ACE**

The ACE gene makes angiotensin converting enzyme, which raises blood pressure by constricting blood vessels. A person's blood pressure response to excess sodium depends on their ACE variant. GG: typical response. GA or AA: elevated blood pressure on high sodium.

**Step 3: consequences**

A salt-sensitive genotype means higher risk of elevated blood pressure on a high sodium intake, and a better response to a low sodium diet.

From: §6

</details>

### Q6: Two menus

Using the slide's two menus, explain why option 1 exceeds the recommendation and option 2 meets it. Which single item contributes most in option 1?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: totals**

Option 1 (sandwich, Caesar with bacon and chicken, crisps, fish and chips): 3750–5350 mg sodium. Option 2 (porridge, plain Caesar, apple, home-made curry): 901–1502 mg.

**Step 2: the target**

6 g salt = 2400 mg sodium. Option 1 is above it, option 2 is within it.

**Step 3: biggest item**

The Caesar salad with chicken and bacon at 1500–2500 mg, then the ham and cheese sandwich at about 1500 mg, of which the two slices of bread are 900 mg.
