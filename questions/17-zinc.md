## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Zinc in the body

How much zinc is in the body, where is it, and what five things is it needed for?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: amount and place**

About 2 g in a 70 kg man. 60% skeletal muscle, 30% bone, 4–6% skin. Only 0.1% in plasma. It is the most abundant intracellular trace element.

**Step 2: needed for**

Growth, reproduction, immune function, gene expression, protein synthesis.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Absorption

Describe zinc absorption: how much, what helps, what hinders, the gut storage protein, how it travels in blood, and how it leaves the body.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: how much**

20–40%, similar to iron.

**Step 2: helps and hinders**

Hinders: phytate. Helps: certain amino acids and organic acids.

**Step 3: store and transport**

Stored in the gut cell as metallothionein. Carried in blood by albumin and α2-macroglobulin.

**Step 4: leaves**

Intestine 1–3 mg/day in shed cells and pancreatic secretions. Urine 0.5 mg/day.

From: §2

</details>

### Q3: Requirements and sources

Give the Irish RDAs for zinc, the status marker, the best foods, and the fact about red meat versus chicken. Why are grains a poor source?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: RDAs**

Men 9.5 mg, women 7 mg.

**Step 2: status**

Plasma zinc concentration.

**Step 3: foods**

Meat, seafood, liver. Lean red meat has 50 mg/kg, twice the chicken content.

**Step 4: grains**

Low bioavailability because of their phytate content.

From: §3

</details>

### Q4: Enzymes

Zinc is a component of more than 100 enzymes. Name its three major enzyme functions and explain them using alcohol dehydrogenase. List five processes that need zinc enzymes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: three functions**

Catalytic (changes reaction rate), structural (stabilises protein folds), regulatory (gene expression).

**Step 2: alcohol dehydrogenase**

It has 4 zinc atoms: 2 for structure and 2 for catalytic activity.

**Step 3: processes**

Alcohol metabolism, protein metabolism, haem synthesis, phospholipid synthesis, antioxidant defence (superoxide dismutase), wound repair, RNA synthesis.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q5: Zinc fingers

What is a transcription factor? Explain how zinc lets a transcription factor control a gene.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: transcription factor**

A protein that binds specific DNA sequences to control the flow of genetic information from DNA to mRNA.

**Step 2: zinc's role**

Zinc binds the transcription factor and changes its shape into a zinc finger. The zinc finger interacts with response elements in DNA to increase or decrease transcription.

From: §5

</details>

---

### Q6: Deficiency

List the signs of zinc deficiency. Who is most at risk and why? What did supplementation trials show, and what is the link to pregnancy?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: signs**

Growth retardation, sexual and skeletal immaturity, neuropsychiatric disturbances, dermatitis, alopecia, diarrhoea, increased infections, loss of appetite.

**Step 2: at risk**

Children in the developing world: high growth needs, low meat intake, high legume intake with phytate.

**Step 3: supplements**

Improved growth, reduced diarrhoea and infections, increased immunity. WHO 2008: supplementation helps treat childhood infectious diseases.

**Step 4: pregnancy**

Deficiency is linked to low birth weight and pre-term delivery.

From: §6

</details>
