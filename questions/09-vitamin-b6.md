## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: The vitamers

Name the three free forms of vitamin B6 and say which chemical group each has.

How many vitamers are there in total, and which is the active coenzyme?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the free forms**

| Form | Group |
|---|---|
| Pyridoxine | alcohol |
| Pyridoxal | aldehyde |
| Pyridoxamine | amine |

**Step 2: the count**

Each has a phosphate form too, so six vitamers in total.

**Step 3: the active one**

Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP).

### Q2: Phosphate on and off

Which enzyme adds a phosphate to vitamin B6? Which one removes it?

Why does phosphorylation keep B6 inside a cell?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the enzymes**

Kinase adds the phosphate. Phosphatase removes it.

An oxidase converts the other forms to PLP.

**Step 2: why it traps**

Phosphorylated forms cannot cross cell membranes. So once the liver or a muscle cell phosphorylates B6, it stays inside.

Tissue levels are set by the balance of phosphorylation and dephosphorylation.

From: §1, §3

</details>

---

### Q3: Absorption and transport

Describe how vitamin B6 gets from food to a muscle cell as PLP.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: gut**

Absorbed in the small intestine in the free, dephosphorylated form.

**Step 2: blood**

Carried bound to albumin.

**Step 3: liver**

Most is taken up by the liver. It is phosphorylated, then oxidised to pyridoxal phosphate. It is exported bound to albumin.

**Step 4: muscle**

80% of body B6 ends up in muscle as PLP bound to glycogen phosphorylase.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q4: Transamination

Explain what PLP does in a transamination. Give the example reaction from the slides and say why this matters for making amino acids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the job**

PLP is the coenzyme to transaminases. It carries the amino group and transfers it to an α-ketoacid.

**Step 2: the example**

Alanine + α-ketoglutarate → pyruvate + glutamate.

The amino group moves from alanine to α-ketoglutarate.

**Step 3: why it matters**

This is how the body makes non-essential amino acids.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q5: The three areas

List the three main areas of metabolism where PLP works. For each, name one specific example from the slides.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Amino acid reactions. Transamination (alanine + α-ketoglutarate → pyruvate + glutamate) and decarboxylation (glutamate → GABA).
2. Cofactor of glycogen phosphorylase, the enzyme that breaks down glycogen.
3. Regulation of steroid hormones. PLP removes the hormone-receptor complex from DNA binding, which terminates the hormone's action.

From: §4, §5, §6

</details>

---

### Q6: Deficiency

Why is severe vitamin B6 deficiency rare?

What happens in moderate deficiency, and what symptoms result?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: why rare**

B6 is widely distributed in foods.

**Step 2: moderate deficiency**

Two changes: abnormal amino acid metabolism, and increased sensitivity to steroid hormone action.

**Step 3: symptoms**

Mood changes, skin and mucosal issues, mild anaemia, reduced immune function.

Summary slide: dermatitis, irritability, anaemia.

From: §7

</details>
