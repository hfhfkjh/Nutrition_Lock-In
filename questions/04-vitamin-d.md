## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: The pathway

Write out the pathway from skin to active vitamin D. Name the starting compound, the two organs, the two enzymes and the two products.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: skin**

7-dehydrocholesterol + UVB → vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol).

**Step 2: liver**

25-hydroxylase adds an OH → 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol).

**Step 3: kidney**

1-hydroxylase adds an OH → 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol). This is the active form.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q2: Two forms

Name the two forms of vitamin D, say where each comes from, and say whether they are metabolised differently.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

D2 = ergocalciferol. Plants and UV-treated mushrooms.

D3 = cholecalciferol. Made in skin, found in animal foods.

Both go through the same metabolism in liver and kidney.

From: §1, §2

</details>

### Q3: IU and micrograms

a. A supplement contains 600 IU of vitamin D. How many µg is that?

b. The FSAI recommends 20 µg a day for housebound older adults. How many IU is that?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: IU to µg**

µg = IU ÷ 40 = 600 ÷ 40 = **15 µg**

**Step 2: µg to IU**

IU = 40 × µg = 40 × 20 = **800 IU**

From: §8, Equation 1

</details>

### Q4: Regulation

Blood calcium drops. Explain what happens to vitamin D metabolism, and how the system switches itself off again.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: turn on**

Low calcium → PTH is secreted.

PTH increases 1-hydroxylase in the kidney, so more 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is made.

**Step 2: turn off**

1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D inhibits its own synthesis.

It also increases 24-hydroxylase, which makes 24-hydroxy calcidiol instead of the active form.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q5: Calcium homeostasis

List the three ways active vitamin D raises blood calcium.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Increases intestinal absorption of calcium.
2. Increases reabsorption in the kidney, so less calcium is excreted.
3. Mobilises bone mineral.

From: §5

</details>

---

### Q6: Deficiency and status

a. Name the deficiency disease in children and in adults, and say what happens to the bones in each.

b. What is measured in blood to assess vitamin D status? Why is Irish winter status poor?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** Children: rickets. Bones fail to calcify normally and bend.
Adults: osteomalacia. Poor mineralisation and demineralisation. Bones are soft, flexible, brittle and deformed. Women and older people most affected.

**b.** 25-hydroxyvitamin D (the liver product), in nmol/L.
Ireland is above 40° N, so there is little UVB in winter. In winter 55% of Irish adults are below 50 nmol/L.

From: §7, §4

</details>

---

### Q7: Toxicity

What does vitamin D toxicity do to the body? Give the intakes linked to toxicity. Can sunlight cause it?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Toxicity raises blood calcium. That increases blood pressure and causes calcification of soft tissue.

Threshold unknown. Adults on supplements above 250 µg per day, infants above 50 µg per day. US UL is 100 µg.

Sunlight does not cause toxicity. It tops out at about 20,000 IU, giving 100 to 200 nmol/L.

From: §9

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Vitamin D3 is cholecalciferol.
2. The kidney converts vitamin D to 25-hydroxyvitamin D.
3. 25-hydroxyvitamin D is the biomarker used to measure vitamin D status.
4. Excess sun exposure can cause vitamin D toxicity.
5. Osteomalacia is the vitamin D deficiency disease of children.
6. Vitamin D is a seco-steroid because one of its rings is broken.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **True.** D2 is ergocalciferol. (§1)
2. **False.** The liver makes 25-hydroxyvitamin D. The kidney makes 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D. (§3)
3. **True.** (§7)
4. **False.** Sun exposure tops out at about 20,000 IU and does not cause toxicity. (§9)
5. **False.** Rickets is in children. Osteomalacia is in adults. (§7)
6. **True.** (§1)

</details>
