## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Vitamins versus minerals

Give two differences between a vitamin and a mineral. Then state the one thing they have in common.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: differences**

Vitamins are organic compounds. Minerals are inorganic elements.

Minerals are never made in the body. Most vitamins are not made either, but a few can be (vitamin D from sunlight, niacin from tryptophan).

**Step 2: in common**

Both must come from the diet, in small amounts.

From: §1

</details>

---

### Q2: The 100 mg rule

What separates a major mineral from a trace element? List the seven major minerals.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the rule**

A major mineral is needed at more than 100 mg per day. A trace element is needed at less than 100 mg per day.

**Step 2: the seven**

Calcium, magnesium, phosphorus, chloride, potassium, sodium, sulfur.

From: §2

</details>

---

### Q3: Absorption

How much digestion do minerals need before absorption? Where are they absorbed? Name two things that change their bioavailability.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: digestion**

Very little. They are already single elements.

**Step 2: where**

Mostly in the small intestine.

**Step 3: bioavailability**

The person's nutrition status, and other nutrients in the meal.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q4: Major versus trace sources

How do food sources of trace elements differ from food sources of major minerals? Give two points.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: major minerals**

Best sources are seafood, meat and dairy. Some vegetables, legumes and whole grains are good.

**Step 2: trace elements**

Found in all food groups. The amount depends on the mineral content of the soil. Not affected by cooking.

From: §3, §4

</details>

---

### Q5: Functions

List the seven critical micronutrient functions from the slides and give one mineral for each of four of them.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the list**

Bone health, antioxidants, oxygen transport, enzyme function, immune function, electrolytes in body fluids, other tissue functions.

**Step 2: examples**

Bone: calcium. Antioxidant: selenium. Oxygen transport: iron. Electrolyte: sodium.

From: §5

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Minerals are organic compounds.
2. A trace element is one needed at less than 100 mg per day.
3. Sulfur is a trace element.
4. Cooking reduces the trace element content of food.
5. Mineral toxicity is common from normal food.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **False.** Minerals are inorganic elements. (§1)
2. **True.** (§2)
3. **False.** Sulfur is one of the seven major minerals. (§2)
4. **False.** Trace element content is not influenced by cooking. (§4)
5. **False.** Toxicity is rare and usually from supplements. (§3)

</details>
