### Q2: Thiamin requirement from energy intake

A student eats 2500 kcal a day.

Convert this to MJ. Then find the range of thiamin PRI for this energy intake.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: convert to MJ**

1 kcal = 0.0042 MJ.

2500 × 0.0042 = **10.5 MJ**

**Step 2: low end of the PRI**

0.07 × 10.5 = **0.74 mg**

**Step 3: high end of the PRI**

0.1 × 10.5 = **1.05 mg**

So the PRI is between 0.74 and 1.05 mg a day.

From: §3, Equation 1

</details>

### Q3: Structure and coenzyme

Describe the structure of thiamin. What is the active coenzyme called, and what are its two names?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: structure**

A pyrimidine ring and a thiazole ring joined by a methylene bridge.

A hydroxyethyl side chain hangs off the thiazole ring.

**Step 2: coenzyme**

Thiamine diphosphate (TDP). Also called thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP). Same molecule.

Two phosphates are added to the side chain.

From: §2

</details>

### Q4: Three functions

Give the three functions of thiamin. For each, say whether thiamin acts as a coenzyme, and name the enzyme or protein involved.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Function | Coenzyme? | Enzyme or protein |
|---|---|---|
| Energy metabolism | Yes, TDP | Oxidative decarboxylation enzymes: pyruvate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase |
| DNA/RNA and NADPH synthesis | Yes, TDP | Transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway |
| Nerve conduction | No | Nerve membrane Na transport protein |

From: §4, §5

</details>

### Q5: The TDP enzymes

Name the three TDP-dependent enzymes and write the reaction each one does. Which other B vitamins are needed by these reactions?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: the enzymes**

1. Pyruvate dehydrogenase: pyruvate → acetyl-CoA.
2. α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase: α-ketoglutarate → succinyl-CoA.
3. Branched-chain keto-acid dehydrogenase: breakdown of branched-chain amino acids.

**Step 2: other vitamins**

B2 riboflavin as FAD. B3 niacin as NAD. B5 pantothenic acid as CoA.

From: §5

</details>

### Q6: Beriberi and Wernicke-Korsakoff

a. What diet pattern leads to beriberi?

b. Compare dry and wet beriberi: which system, which signs.

c. What is Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, who gets it, and why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** Chronic thiamin deficiency together with a high carbohydrate intake.

**b.** Dry beriberi = nervous system. Weakness, stiffness and cramps in the legs, then muscle wasting and paralysis spreading upward.
Wet beriberi = heart. Arterioles dilate, fast blood flow and pulse, heart failure and oedema.

**c.** Thiamin deficiency in alcoholics, with lesions in the central nervous system. Korsakoff's psychosis = loss of recent memory and confusion. Wernicke's encephalopathy = affects vision.
Alcoholics get it because of low thiamin intake, high carbohydrate intake, poor absorption and liver damage.

From: §6, §7

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Thiamin is a fat-soluble vitamin.
2. The coenzyme form of thiamin is thiamine diphosphate.
3. The thiamin requirement is expressed per MJ of energy intake.
4. Unenriched white rice contains more thiamin than brown rice.
5. Wet beriberi affects the heart.
6. Thiamin's role in nerve conduction is a coenzyme role.

4. **False.** Brown rice 0.19 mg, white unenriched 0.04 mg. (§3)

6. **False.** It is a non-coenzyme role. (§4)
