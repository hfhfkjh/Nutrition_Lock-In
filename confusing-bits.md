# Confusing bits

Things that are easy to mix up in an MCQ, with the short answer.

Add your own as you go. Re-read this before the exam.

## Topic 1 — Basics

**Q: PRI, AR, AI, RDA, UL. Which is which?**
- **AR** = average requirement. Enough for **half** the people.
- **PRI** = population reference intake. Enough for **virtually all**. (The EFSA name for an RDA.)
- **AI** = adequate intake. Used when nobody can work out an AR. It is the average intake of a healthy group.
- **UL** = upper limit. The most you can take safely.
- 📍 Notes: [01 §3](notes/01-vitamin-basics.md)

**Q: Why are fat-soluble vitamins more likely to be toxic?**
- They are stored in fat and the liver and are not easily excreted. Water-soluble extras go out in the urine.
- 📍 Notes: [01 §2](notes/01-vitamin-basics.md)

## Topic 2 — Vitamin A

**Q: Retinol, retinal, retinoic acid. Which does what?**
- Retin**al** = vision (part of rhodopsin).
- Retinoic **acid** = gene expression (binds RAR and RXR on the RARE).
- Retin**ol** = the transport and storage form, the alcohol.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §1, §5, §6](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

**Q: Chylomicrons or retinol binding protein?**
- Chylomicrons carry vitamin A **from the gut to the liver**.
- RBP (with transthyretin) carries it **out of the liver** into the blood.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §4](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

**Q: Xerophthalmia or night blindness: which comes first?**
- Night blindness first. Then xerophthalmia (dry eye, Bitot's spots). Then blindness.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §7](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

## Topic 3 — Vitamin K

**Q: K1, K2, K3?**
- K1 phylloquinone = plants (green leaves). K2 menaquinone = gut bacteria. K3 menadiol = synthetic.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §1](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

**Q: What is Gla?**
- γ-carboxyglutamate. Vitamin K helps add a carboxyl group to glutamate in a protein. The Gla then binds calcium. That activates clotting factors and bone proteins.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §3](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

**Q: Is vitamin K toxic?**
- No evidence of toxicity, no UL. Only the synthetic form has known toxicity. High doses do reduce the effect of warfarin.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §7](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

## Topic 4 — Vitamin D

**Q: 25-hydroxy or 1,25-dihydroxy?**
- **25-hydroxyvitamin D** is made in the **liver**. It is the **biomarker** of status.
- **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D** (calcitriol) is made in the **kidney**. It is the **active** hormone.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §3, §7](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: Rickets or osteomalacia?**
- Rickets = children, bones fail to calcify and bend.
- Osteomalacia = adults, bones demineralise, soft and brittle. Women and older people.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §7](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: Can you get vitamin D toxicity from the sun?**
- No. Sunlight tops out at about 20,000 IU. Toxicity comes from supplements.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §9](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: D2 or D3?**
- D2 ergocalciferol (plants, UV mushrooms). D3 cholecalciferol (skin, animal foods). Same metabolism.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §1](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

## Topic 5 — Vitamin E

**Q: Does vitamin E get used up?**
- It becomes the tocopheroxyl radical after donating an electron. Vitamin C turns it back into tocopherol.
- 📍 Notes: [05 §6](notes/05-vitamin-e.md)

**Q: Do vitamin E supplements prevent heart disease?**
- Observational studies say high blood vitamin E goes with less CVD. Randomised trials of high-dose supplements were disappointing: small or no effect, sometimes more risk.
- 📍 Notes: [05 §8](notes/05-vitamin-e.md)

## Topics 6 to 8 — B1, B2, B3, B5

**Q: Which coenzyme belongs to which vitamin?**
- B1 → TDP (TPP). B2 → FAD, FMN. B3 → NAD, NADP. B5 → CoA. B6 → PLP. B7 → biotin on carboxylases. B9 → THF.
- 📍 Cheat sheet: [cheat-sheet.md](cheat-sheet.md)

**Q: Dry or wet beriberi?**
- Dry = nervous system (legs, wasting, paralysis). Wet = heart (fast pulse, heart failure, oedema).
- 📍 Notes: [06 §6](notes/06-thiamin.md)

**Q: Wernicke or Korsakoff?**
- Korsakoff's psychosis = memory loss and confusion. Wernicke's encephalopathy = vision. Both from thiamin deficiency in alcoholics.
- 📍 Notes: [06 §7](notes/06-thiamin.md)

**Q: Why does a maize diet cause pellagra?**
- Maize is low in tryptophan (the niacin precursor) and its own niacin is bound and unavailable.
- 📍 Notes: [07 §6, §8](notes/07-riboflavin-niacin.md)

**Q: Which vitamins does pyruvate dehydrogenase need?**
- B1 (TDP), B2 (FAD), B3 (NAD) and B5 (CoA). All four energy vitamins at one enzyme.
- 📍 Notes: [06 §5](notes/06-thiamin.md)

## Topics 9 to 11 — B6, B7, B9

**Q: Why does B6 need scale with protein?**
- PLP is the coenzyme for transamination and amino acid metabolism. More protein, more amino acids to handle. 15 µg per g protein.
- 📍 Notes: [09 §2, §4](notes/09-vitamin-b6.md)

**Q: Raw eggs and biotin?**
- Avidin in raw egg **white** binds biotin and blocks absorption. Cooking fixes it.
- 📍 Notes: [10 §4](notes/10-biotin.md)

**Q: Folate or folic acid?**
- Folate = natural, in food, mostly polyglutamates, about 50% available.
- Folic acid = synthetic, in supplements and fortified foods, about 100% available. That is why it counts 1.7× in DFE.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §1, §3](notes/11-folate.md)

**Q: Why anaemia from folate deficiency?**
- Red cell precursors divide fast and need thymidine (dTMP) for DNA. No folate, no dTMP, DNA strands break, cells stay large: megaloblastic anaemia.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §6, §7](notes/11-folate.md)

**Q: 400 µg or 4 mg?**
- 400 µg a day for every woman who could become pregnant, before conception and for the first 12 weeks.
- 4 mg (4000 µg) only to prevent a **recurrence** after a previous NTD.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §9](notes/11-folate.md)

## Topics 12 to 14 — Minerals, calcium, phosphorus, magnesium

**Q: Major or trace?**
- More than 100 mg a day = major: Ca, Mg, P, Cl, K, Na, S. Less = trace: Fe, I, Zn, Se, Cu, F, Mn, Cr, Co, Mo.
- 📍 Notes: [12 §2](notes/12-mineral-basics.md)

**Q: Absorption, reabsorption, resorption?**
- Absorption = gut → blood. Reabsorption = kidney filtrate → blood. Resorption = bone → blood.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §5](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: PTH or calcitonin?**
- PTH is released when calcium is **low** and raises it: more absorption (via vitamin D), more reabsorption, more resorption.
- Calcitonin is released when calcium is **high** and lowers it: less of all three.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §5](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: Calcitriol, calbindin, calcitonin?**
- Calcitriol = active vitamin D (1,25-dihydroxy). Calbindin = the gut transport protein calcitriol switches on. Calcitonin = the calcium-lowering hormone.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §4, §5](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: Active or passive calcium absorption?**
- Active = vitamin D dependent, through the cell, about 20–30%. Passive = concentration dependent, between cells, about 50–70%. Overall about 30% of intake gets in.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §3](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: Osteopenia or osteoporosis?**
- Osteopenia: BMD between −1 and −2.5 SD of the young adult mean. Osteoporosis: below −2.5 SD.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §8](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: Why is plasma calcium useless as a status test?**
- It is held at 9–10.4 mg/100 ml whatever you eat, because bone gives up calcium. Use DEXA.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §8, §9](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: 800, 1000 or 1200 mg calcium?**
- Irish RDA: adults 800, teens and pregnancy 1200. EFSA PRI: 1000.
- 📍 Notes: [13 §2](notes/13-calcium.md)

**Q: Phosphorus RDA or calcium RDA?**
- Phosphorus 550 mg adults. Calcium 800 mg. Both go up in pregnancy (950 and 1200).
- 📍 Notes: [14 §2](notes/14-phosphorus-magnesium.md)

**Q: Why is the magnesium UL lower than the AI?**
- UL 250 mg applies to supplements only. Food magnesium has never caused toxicity. AI is 300–350 mg.
- 📍 Notes: [14 §9](notes/14-phosphorus-magnesium.md)

## Topics 15 to 18 — Iron, iodine, zinc, selenium

**Q: Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺?**
- Fe²⁺ ferrous is absorbed. Fe³⁺ ferric must be reduced first. Vitamin C does the reducing.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §2, §5](notes/15-iron.md)

**Q: Ferritin, transferrin, haemosiderin, hepcidin?**
- Ferritin = store (20%). Haemosiderin = long-term store (10%). Transferrin = transport (< 1%). Hepcidin = the liver hormone that turns absorption down when stores are high.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §3, §4](notes/15-iron.md)

**Q: HCP1 or DMT1?**
- HCP1 carries haem iron in intact. DMT1 carries non-haem Fe²⁺.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §4](notes/15-iron.md)

**Q: 16 or 11? 14 or 10?**
- EFSA PRI: women 16, men 11 mg. The other table (RDA): women 14, men 10. Women always higher.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §2, §8](notes/15-iron.md)

**Q: Which falls first in iron deficiency?**
- Ferritin (stage 1, < 12 µg/L). Then serum iron down and binding capacity up (stage 2). Then haemoglobin down and protoporphyrin up (stage 3).
- 📍 Notes: [15 §9](notes/15-iron.md)

**Q: Small pale cells or large cells?**
- Small and pale (microcytic, hypochromic) = iron. Large (megaloblastic) = folate.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §9](notes/15-iron.md), [11 §7](notes/11-folate.md)

**Q: Goitre or cretinism?**
- Goitre: the adult thyroid swells because TSH is high. Cretinism: the baby of a deficient mother, permanent mental impairment.
- 📍 Notes: [16 §4](notes/16-iodine.md)

**Q: Hypo- or hyperthyroidism, which has high TSH?**
- Hypothyroidism: high TSH, low T4 and T3 (the pituitary keeps shouting). Hyperthyroidism: excess T4 and T3.
- 📍 Notes: [16 §6](notes/16-iodine.md)

**Q: TRH, TSH, T3, T4?**
- Hypothalamus TRH → pituitary TSH → thyroid T3 (3 iodines) and T4 (4 iodines). T3/T4 feed back and switch the first two off.
- 📍 Notes: [16 §3](notes/16-iodine.md)

**Q: Why no iodised salt in Ireland?**
- It is not mandatory, less than 5% of salt is iodised. So milk is the Irish iodine source and status drops in summer.
- 📍 Notes: [16 §2](notes/16-iodine.md)

**Q: Metallothionein or ferritin?**
- Both are temporary stores in the gut cell. Metallothionein for zinc, ferritin for iron.
- 📍 Notes: [17 §2](notes/17-zinc.md)

**Q: Superoxide dismutase or glutathione peroxidase?**
- Superoxide dismutase = zinc. Glutathione peroxidase = selenium (with vitamin E).
- 📍 Notes: [17 §4](notes/17-zinc.md), [18 §3](notes/18-selenium.md)

**Q: Keshan or Kashin-Beck?**
- Keshan = heart (cardiomyopathy). Kashin-Beck = bones and joints (osteoarthritis). Both selenium, both children.
- 📍 Notes: [18 §4](notes/18-selenium.md)

**Q: Which nutrients does phytate block?**
- Calcium, non-haem iron, zinc. Not the fat-soluble vitamins.
- 📍 Notes: [15 §5](notes/15-iron.md), [17 §2](notes/17-zinc.md)

## Topics 19 and 20 — Electrolytes and salt

**Q: Sodium or potassium inside the cell?**
- Potassium inside (150 mM). Sodium outside (142 mM). The pump sends 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in.
- 📍 Notes: [19 §2](notes/19-electrolytes.md)

**Q: Does aldosterone keep or lose sodium?**
- Keeps sodium, loses potassium. It rises when blood sodium is low or blood pressure is low (via RAAS).
- 📍 Notes: [19 §6, §8](notes/19-electrolytes.md)

**Q: Where is renin, where is ACE?**
- Renin from the kidney. Angiotensinogen from the liver. ACE in the lungs. Aldosterone from the adrenals.
- 📍 Notes: [19 §7](notes/19-electrolytes.md)

**Q: 0.4 or 2.54?**
- Salt → sodium: multiply by 0.4. Sodium → salt (labels): multiply by 2.54.
- 📍 Notes: [19 §3](notes/19-electrolytes.md), [20 §2](notes/20-salt-and-health.md)

**Q: 6 g, 2.4 g, 11 g?**
- 6 g salt is the target. 2.4 g is the sodium in it. 11 g is what Irish men actually eat (women 8.5 g).
- 📍 Notes: [20 §2](notes/20-salt-and-health.md)

**Q: Where does most salt come from?**
- Manufactured foods, 65–70%. Not the salt cellar (15–20%). Bread is the single biggest food group.
- 📍 Notes: [20 §2, §3](notes/20-salt-and-health.md)

**Q: GG or GA/AA?**
- GG = typical blood pressure on high sodium. GA or AA = elevated, and these people respond best to cutting salt.
- 📍 Notes: [20 §6](notes/20-salt-and-health.md)
