# Confusing bits

Things that are easy to mix up in an MCQ, with the short answer.

## Topic 1 — Basics

**Q: PRI, AR, AI, RDA, UL. Which is which?**
- **AR** = average requirement. Enough for **half** the people.
- **PRI** = population reference intake. Enough for **virtually all**. (The EFSA name for an RDA.)
- **AI** = adequate intake. Used when nobody can work out an AR. It is the average intake of a healthy group.
- **UL** = upper limit. The most you can take safely.
- 📍 Notes: [01 §3](notes/01-vitamin-basics.md)

**Q: Why are fat-soluble vitamins more likely to be toxic?**
- They are stored in fat and the liver and are not easily excreted. Water-soluble extras go out in the urine.
- 📍 Notes: [01 §2](notes/01-vitamin-basics.md)

## Topic 2 — Vitamin A

**Q: Retinol, retinal, retinoic acid. Which does what?**
- Retin**al** = vision (part of rhodopsin).
- Retinoic **acid** = gene expression (binds RAR and RXR on the RARE).
- Retin**ol** = the transport and storage form, the alcohol.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §1, §5, §6](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

**Q: Chylomicrons or retinol binding protein?**
- Chylomicrons carry vitamin A **from the gut to the liver**.
- RBP (with transthyretin) carries it **out of the liver** into the blood.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §4](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

**Q: Xerophthalmia or night blindness: which comes first?**
- Night blindness first. Then xerophthalmia (dry eye, Bitot's spots). Then blindness.
- 📍 Notes: [02 §7](notes/02-vitamin-a.md)

## Topic 3 — Vitamin K

**Q: K1, K2, K3?**
- K1 phylloquinone = plants (green leaves). K2 menaquinone = gut bacteria. K3 menadiol = synthetic.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §1](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

**Q: What is Gla?**
- γ-carboxyglutamate. Vitamin K helps add a carboxyl group to glutamate in a protein. The Gla then binds calcium. That activates clotting factors and bone proteins.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §3](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

**Q: Is vitamin K toxic?**
- No evidence of toxicity, no UL. Only the synthetic form has known toxicity. High doses do reduce the effect of warfarin.
- 📍 Notes: [03 §7](notes/03-vitamin-k.md)

## Topic 4 — Vitamin D

**Q: 25-hydroxy or 1,25-dihydroxy?**
- **25-hydroxyvitamin D** is made in the **liver**. It is the **biomarker** of status.
- **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D** (calcitriol) is made in the **kidney**. It is the **active** hormone.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §3, §7](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: Rickets or osteomalacia?**
- Rickets = children, bones fail to calcify and bend.
- Osteomalacia = adults, bones demineralise, soft and brittle. Women and older people.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §7](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: Can you get vitamin D toxicity from the sun?**
- No. Sunlight tops out at about 20,000 IU. Toxicity comes from supplements.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §9](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

**Q: D2 or D3?**
- D2 ergocalciferol (plants, UV mushrooms). D3 cholecalciferol (skin, animal foods). Same metabolism.
- 📍 Notes: [04 §1](notes/04-vitamin-d.md)

## Topic 5 — Vitamin E

**Q: Does vitamin E get used up?**
- It becomes the tocopheroxyl radical after donating an electron. Vitamin C turns it back into tocopherol.
- 📍 Notes: [05 §6](notes/05-vitamin-e.md)

**Q: Do vitamin E supplements prevent heart disease?**
- Observational studies say high blood vitamin E goes with less CVD. Randomised trials of high-dose supplements were disappointing: small or no effect, sometimes more risk.
- 📍 Notes: [05 §8](notes/05-vitamin-e.md)

## Topics 6 to 8 — B1, B2, B3, B5

**Q: Which coenzyme belongs to which vitamin?**
- B1 → TDP (TPP). B2 → FAD, FMN. B3 → NAD, NADP. B5 → CoA. B6 → PLP. B7 → biotin on carboxylases. B9 → THF.
- 📍 Cheat sheet: [cheat-sheet.md](cheat-sheet.md)

**Q: Dry or wet beriberi?**
- Dry = nervous system (legs, wasting, paralysis). Wet = heart (fast pulse, heart failure, oedema).
- 📍 Notes: [06 §6](notes/06-thiamin.md)

**Q: Wernicke or Korsakoff?**
- Korsakoff's psychosis = memory loss and confusion. Wernicke's encephalopathy = vision. Both from thiamin deficiency in alcoholics.
- 📍 Notes: [06 §7](notes/06-thiamin.md)

**Q: Why does a maize diet cause pellagra?**
- Maize is low in tryptophan (the niacin precursor) and its own niacin is bound and unavailable.
- 📍 Notes: [07 §6, §8](notes/07-riboflavin-niacin.md)

**Q: Which vitamins does pyruvate dehydrogenase need?**
- B1 (TDP), B2 (FAD), B3 (NAD) and B5 (CoA). All four energy vitamins at one enzyme.
- 📍 Notes: [06 §5](notes/06-thiamin.md)

## Topics 9 to 11 — B6, B7, B9

**Q: Why does B6 need scale with protein?**
- PLP is the coenzyme for transamination and amino acid metabolism. More protein, more amino acids to handle. 15 µg per g protein.
- 📍 Notes: [09 §2, §4](notes/09-vitamin-b6.md)

**Q: Raw eggs and biotin?**
- Avidin in raw egg **white** binds biotin and blocks absorption. Cooking fixes it.
- 📍 Notes: [10 §4](notes/10-biotin.md)

**Q: Folate or folic acid?**
- Folate = natural, in food, mostly polyglutamates, about 50% available.
- Folic acid = synthetic, in supplements and fortified foods, about 100% available. That is why it counts 1.7× in DFE.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §1, §3](notes/11-folate.md)

**Q: Why anaemia from folate deficiency?**
- Red cell precursors divide fast and need thymidine (dTMP) for DNA. No folate, no dTMP, DNA strands break, cells stay large: megaloblastic anaemia.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §6, §7](notes/11-folate.md)

**Q: 400 µg or 4 mg?**
- 400 µg a day for every woman who could become pregnant, before conception and for the first 12 weeks.
- 4 mg (4000 µg) only to prevent a **recurrence** after a previous NTD.
- 📍 Notes: [11 §9](notes/11-folate.md)
